01 / INFORMATION DESIGN2026 · JAN–APR

Find the way
through.

Reorganizing DFO Fish Habitat information so people can learn, take action, and find research without getting lost.

  • Content inventory
  • Card sorting
  • Tree testing
Read the case study+

As researcher and documentation specialist on a five-person team, I helped audit the DFO site and test its information architecture. A 20-card sort with 13 participants showed how strongly labels shaped where people expected to find information.

What changed After restructuring the site into five clear areas, invasive-species reporting success rose from 30% to 60%. Lost-gear task time fell from 41.6 to 25.8 seconds, while success rose from 80% to 87.5%.

02 / BROCHURE DESIGN2024 · SEP–DEC

A guide to
belonging.

A print-ready guide connecting Laurier students with Brantford places, transit, services, and student discounts.

  • 13 student interviews
  • Prototyping
  • Usability testing
Read the case study+

We began with an app concept, then 13 student interviews surfaced barriers around awareness, getting around, safety, and going out alone. With the client, we shifted to a physical guide delivered at Orientation Week, where incoming students could use it throughout the year.

What changed Three usability rounds with 12 participants shaped a double-fold, print-ready brochure with local listings, a transit map, One Card details, discounts, and a coupon strip.

03 / APP DESIGN2026 · JAN–APR

One union.
One place.

Bringing student union services, events, and community into one experience, guided by how students already seek information.

  • Student interviews
  • Survey research
  • Usability testing
Read the case study+

Research revealed a fragmented experience across the Students’ Union website, Instagram, and email. While 72% of surveyed students mainly knew the SU through Instagram, only 39% said its services were clear, and 17% had never heard of the website.

Design direction We explored an app that brings events, services, community, and student identity together. Usability testing highlighted grouped navigation, the events calendar, OneCard access, and involvement points.

A WORKING POINT OF VIEW

Good design
is a practice.

Not a finish line. A set of choices, made with care, checked with real people, and improved over time.

    Start with the person

    Listen before solving. Context is part of the interface.
